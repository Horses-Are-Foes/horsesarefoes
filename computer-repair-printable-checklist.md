# Computer Repair Company — Start Checklist
Print this and check each box as you go. Order is roughly sequential, but items in the same group can run in parallel.

## 1. Plan & Validate
- [ ] Write a one-page business plan (services, pricing, customers, costs)
- [ ] Pick your niche (laptops, desktops, Macs, data recovery, networking, small business IT)
- [ ] Research 3–5 local competitors and their pricing
- [ ] Decide: side gig vs. full-time; mobile (on-site) vs. shop vs. both
- [ ] Set a launch date and a realistic budget

## 2. Legal & Administrative
- [ ] Choose a structure (solo → LLC recommended for liability protection)
- [ ] Pick and register a business name (check availability + domain)
- [ ] Get an EIN (free from the IRS)
- [ ] Open a separate business bank account
- [ ] Register for local/state business licenses and sales tax (if selling parts)
- [ ] Buy general liability insurance (+ cyber/data coverage)
- [ ] Create a signed repair waiver / liability form for every job

## 3. Skills & Credibility
- [ ] Verify your own skill level; fill gaps
- [ ] Consider CompTIA A+ certification (strongly signals trust)
- [ ] Optional: Network+, Security+, or manufacturer certs
- [ ] Build a portfolio: offer free/cheap repairs to friends/family for before+after proof

## 4. Tools & Equipment
- [ ] ESD-safe toolkit (precision screwdrivers, tweezers, spudgers, pliers)
- [ ] Anti-static mat + wrist strap
- [ ] Multimeter and diagnostic tools
- [ ] Bootable USB with free diagnostic/repair software
- [ ] Consumables: compressed air, isopropyl alcohol, thermal paste
- [ ] Starter spare-parts inventory (RAM, SSDs/HDDs, cables, batteries, chargers)

## 5. Workspace & Vehicle
- [ ] Set up a clean, well-lit, organized workbench
- [ ] Get storage bins / labeling for parts and completed jobs
- [ ] Arrange transport for pick-ups/deliveries (bike, car, e-bike)
- [ ] (Later) evaluate a branded service vehicle for marketing + capacity

## 6. Software & Operations
- [ ] Pick a ticketing/repair-management tool (track device + status + customer)
- [ ] Set up invoicing + card payments (e.g., Square)
- [ ] Standardize your service menu and price list
- [ ] Write a clear data-handling / backup & privacy policy
- [ ] Create templates: quotes, invoices, repair waivers, follow-up emails

## 7. Marketing & Launch
- [ ] Build a simple professional website (services, pricing, contact, booking)
- [ ] Create and VERIFY your Google Business Profile (most important local tool)
- [ ] Set up Facebook + a Nextdoor presence
- [ ] Design business cards + flyers; hand them to local shops, libraries, colleges
- [ ] Launch with a promotion (e.g., 10% off first repair, free diagnostics week)
- [ ] Soft-launch: do first few jobs (discounted) to generate real reviews

## 8. Growth & Retention
- [ ] Actively request Google reviews after every happy customer
- [ ] Add recurring revenue: maintenance plans, small-business IT contracts
- [ ] Track which marketing channels bring customers; double down on what works
- [ ] Consider hiring a second tech or adding data-recovery / reselling refurbished units
- [ ] Set quarterly reviews of pricing, costs, and service mix

---
_Rough starting budget: ~$1,000–$4,000 home-based (see cost estimate file)._
